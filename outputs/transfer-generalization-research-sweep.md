# Research sweep — transfer and generalization

Targeted broad search using alphaXiv, PubMed/Crossref/OpenAlex, ERIC, web search and direct full-text retrieval. Not a registered/systematic review. Search results and primary documents inspected as detailed below. User’s research-contract outcomes tracked separately: taught-item retention; near transfer; far/contextual transfer; conceptual application; procedural flexibility; and delayed outcomes.

## Directly inspected full texts / source records
- Pan & Rickard (2018), *Transfer of Test-Enhanced Learning*, DOI 10.1037/bul0000151; author-hosted 48-page PDF fetched/extracted at `https://pdf.retrievalpractice.org/transfer/Pan_Rickard_2018.pdf`. Meta-analysis: 192 effects/122 experiments/67 articles, N=10,382; overall Cohen’s d=.40, 95% CI [.31,.50]; transfer categories include test format, stimulus-response rearrangement, untested-but-studied material, application/inference, problem solving, and mediator/related cues. Publication-bias sensitivity and moderator estimates summarized in review with caution.
- Barnett & Ceci (2002), *When and Where Do We Apply What We Learn?*, DOI 10.1037/0033-2909.128.4.612; full text fetched at https://pdf.retrievalpractice.org/transfer/Barnett_Ceci_2002.pdf. Nine transfer dimensions; authors caution against one unitary “far transfer” effect.
- Gick & Holyoak (1983), *Schema Induction and Analogical Transfer*, full PDF fetched at https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/1983-gick.pdf. Multiple analogs and schema comparison promote transfer; schema quality predicted solution to semantically distant target. Exact sample estimates not extracted here.
- Gentner, Loewenstein & Thompson (2003), *Learning and Transfer: A General Role for Analogical Encoding*, DOI 10.1037/0022-0663.95.2.393; full PDF fetched. Three novice negotiation studies: comparison vs separate case learning; study reports that comparison enhances abstraction/schema expression and transfer, including face-to-face task. Quantitative details selectively from source search summary.
- National Research Council (2000), *How People Learn*, ch. 3, https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/9853/chapter/6. Direct chapter fetch supports: tests of recall can hide differences in transfer; initial mastery is necessary; over-contextualized knowledge can constrain transfer; transfer is dynamic; transfer should be assessed through novel contexts; several classic transfer examples and case-comparison recommendations.
- Fuchs et al. (2010), DOI 10.1086/651191, PMC full text https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2882678/. Randomized classroom schema-broadening instruction (18 classrooms/270 grade-2 students, 16 weeks) vs business-as-usual; superior word-problem learning and structural equation representations. Paper situates schema broadening across cover story, irrelevant information, format, and combined problem types; avoid claiming far-transfer effect for this study without further outcome-table extraction.
- van Blankenstein et al. (2019), DOI 10.1007/s10459-018-9858-6, PMC https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6373526/. First-year medical physiology course, 317 consented students randomized by seminar to peer instruction vs self-explanation, within-person intervention/control concepts. Immediate posttest favored PI (+.27 vs +.16 points; p=.006, partial eta squared .012). Near > far transfer; no PI-vs-SE transfer difference (B=.004,p=.904). Transfer used 2 near and 2 far MC items per concept; short-term only.
- Banerjee et al. (2025), “Children’s arithmetic skills do not transfer between applied and academic mathematics,” DOI 10.1038/s41586-024-08502-w, *Nature* 639, 673–681. Nature full text fetched and rechecked in Fig. 1a–c/Supplementary Tables 1–2 (Kolkata) and Fig. 2/related tables (Delhi). Field studies of working children and school peers show strong contextual performance and limited portability between market and abstract school representations. Observational group comparisons plus task/setting differences mean not an instructional causal effect or a single standardized transfer metric. Within-market novel tasks show graded performance drops as unfamiliarity/working-memory demands increase.
- Zhang & Zhao (2008), DOI 10.3724/SP.J.1041.2008.00409, source page directly fetched at https://journal.psych.ac.cn/acps/EN/Y2008/V40/I04/409. 210 second graders, six example-variation groups plus control, 15-item word-problem test. Surface variation supported near transfer; structural-variation effects depended on how rules were varied/combined; rule-parallel-combination group did best on the authors’ far-transfer category.
- Morris, Bransford & Franks (1977), DOI 10.1016/S0022-5371(77)80016-9. Crossref metadata verified; findings located in source search: semantic study better for standard recognition, phonemic/rhyme study better for rhyme recognition. Publisher full text not retrieved; use only as TAP illustration, not broad educational transfer effect.
- Pan et al. (2018) and the 2021 “Meta-Analysis of Ten Learning Techniques” retrieved via Frontiers. The latter has 242 studies/1,619 effects/N=169,179, with high heterogeneity and surface/immediate-test dominance; its near/far difference is described in the review but not used as universal technique effect.
- Case-comparison meta-analysis Alfieri et al. (2013), DOI 10.1080/00461520.2013.775712; Crossref metadata; ERIC/source-search result reports 57 experiments/336 tests, overall d=.50 [0.44,.56], and conditional near/far estimates only for subset where principle was given after comparison. Full publisher paper not fetched; label result as synthesis-level and be careful not to call subset estimate an overall effect.

## Additional source leads / limitations
- Rittle-Johnson & Star (2007) equation-solving comparison study (DOI 10.1037/0022-0663.99.3.561): title/DOI/journal/pages confirmed independently in OpenAlex and ERIC; ERIC/abstract-level record supports the 70-student seventh-grade comparison and directional procedural/flexibility result. Primary article PDF was not retrieved; no effect size is used. Mark this as abstract/index-level.
- Perfetto, Bransford & Franks (1983), “Constraints on access in a problem solving context,” DOI 10.3758/BF03197658; full author PDF fetched from Springer direct URL. Classic clue/riddle access failure supports inert knowledge as spontaneous retrieval/use failure; sample/effect details not extracted.
- Bransford & Schwartz (1999), DOI 10.3102/0091732X024001061, abstract metadata; preparation-for-future-learning distinction may contextualize a transfer construct, but not direct causal effect estimate.
- Sala et al. cognitive-training far-transfer second-order meta-analysis, DOI 10.1525/collabra.203 (2019 issue; sometimes cited 2017/2018 as preprint). Treat as a domain-specific caution about cognitive-training generalization, not a universal verdict on educational transfer.
- OpenAlex/Web literature search routes found irrelevant results/occasional rate limits; no source failures interpreted as evidence of null effects.
