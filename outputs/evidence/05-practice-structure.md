# Practice structure and skill acquisition

**Scope.** This review follows `outputs/research-contract.md`. Blocked practice concentrates one task type before switching; interleaved/random practice mixes types; variable practice changes examples/conditions, which is not identical to randomizing task order. Contextual-interference findings are evaluated within task domain. Acquisition/practice performance, delayed retention, transfer, and fluent performance are separated. Motor findings are not assumed to apply to academic tasks.

## Summary

Practice structure is an outcome- and domain-dependent choice. Blocked repetition often improves performance during acquisition and can help establish a new response; interleaving may improve delayed retention or selecting among similar problem types, especially in some mathematical and inductive-learning settings. Academic meta-analysis finds an average interleaving benefit, but task material moderates it: math effects are smaller than some category-learning effects, while language vocabulary and text materials may show nulls or blocking advantages. In motor learning, contextual interference has positive average laboratory retention/transfer findings in recent reviews, but effects are highly heterogeneous and smaller or nonsignificant in applied sport settings; study quality is often weak. Do not generalize this motor effect to classroom learning.

Variable practice, deliberate practice, overlearning, automaticity, and speed/accuracy each concern different dimensions. Deliberate practice is not interchangeable with hours of repetition; estimates of its contribution depend on definition and measurement. Overlearning can support retention after criterion but yields diminishing returns and does not itself establish flexible transfer. Faster performance is not necessarily better learning if accuracy falls. No universal “block first, then interleave” or “always randomize” algorithm is established across all domains.

**Adaptive-engine inference:** represent task family, similarity, practice schedule, response accuracy and speed, feedback, retention delay, and transfer separately. For novel skills, begin with enough structured practice for basic accuracy, then test mixed/variable practice when the target requires discrimination or adaptation. Treat domain-specific rules as hypotheses and evaluate delayed retention and transfer against equal-time baselines.

## Domain-by-domain interpretation

- **Perceptual/category learning:** interleaving can help discrimination when categories are confusable and examples within a category are diverse; choose measures that test classification of new instances. This is not equivalent to motor contextual interference.
- **Mathematics:** classroom evidence supports interleaving taught problem types to improve later method selection and delayed test scores in some middle-school curricula. Keep initial instruction and feedback distinct from later practice order.
- **Conceptual learning:** mixed examples may support category/strategy discrimination, but broad transfer claims require new-context tests; current sources do not show a universal conceptual benefit.
- **Motor skills/sport:** random practice can help retention/transfer in lab tasks on average, but applied-field estimates are weaker and study quality/heterogeneity limit confidence.
- **Language and factual learning:** the academic interleaving meta-analysis does not support a universal win; some word-learning contexts favor blocking. For factual material, practice schedule evidence should be separated from the choice/order of task type; no broad factual interleaving estimate established here.
- **Deliberate practice/overlearning/automaticity:** these address practice quality, extra repetitions after criterion, and performance efficiency, respectively. They should not be merged into a single “more practice” dose.

## Observed findings vs proposed mechanisms

**Observed:** blocked conditions can perform better during practice; interleaving sometimes improves delayed discrimination/retention; effects depend on domain and setting. Deliberate-practice variance estimates differ across operational definitions. Overlearning can support retention, and immediate performance is not a reliable stand-in for long-term learning.

**Proposed mechanisms:** contextual interference theories invoke elaborative/forgetting-and-reconstruction processes; discrimination accounts emphasize comparing categories; deliberate-practice accounts emphasize goal-directed task selection and feedback; automaticity accounts describe reduced attentional demand. These are explanatory proposals and are not uniquely established by the outcome contrasts summarized here.

## Disagreements and open questions

1. How much of the contextual-interference effect is genuine durable learning versus test-format/context effects, and why is the average smaller in applied sport settings?
2. Which mathematical curricula and learner profiles benefit from mixing types after initial instruction, and what amount of blocked introduction is sufficient?
3. Do academic interleaving effects depend on the same discrimination processes as perceptual categorization, or do different mechanisms dominate by task?
4. Which overlearning dose improves long-delay performance enough to justify its opportunity cost, and for which high-consequence components?
5. How should deliberate practice be operationalized so estimates distinguish activity quality from accumulated hours without relying on disputed retrospective measures?
6. Which speed/accuracy metric predicts later flexible performance rather than merely immediate response criterion?

## Next research decisions

- In classroom math, randomize blocked vs interleaved order after common instruction, equalize practice time/feedback, and preregister delayed method-selection and transfer outcomes.
- In motor practice, compare lab and field delivery with matched tasks and quality assessment to isolate setting from age/skill.
- Test variable-practice dose and overlearning after a common mastery criterion; measure delayed retention, transfer, speed, and error jointly.
- Report deliberate-practice activities and measurement reliability separately from practice hours and prior ability.
