## Summary

The draft offers a targeted, appropriately qualified synthesis of transfer research spanning taxonomies, meta-analyses, laboratory analogy and memory experiments, classroom mathematics, medical education, and market arithmetic. It distinguishes retention from transfer, separates observed findings from instructional inference in its table, and repeatedly cautions against treating source-specific “near/far” labels as interchangeable. Its strongest practical contribution is an assessment-oriented framework emphasizing changed task dimensions, cueing, delay, and matched formats.

The main revision need is evidence traceability and tighter calibration of claims to access level. Most notably, the Nunes et al. (2025) title in the bibliography is wrong; some reported numbers and primary-study details are explicitly search/abstract-level or not independently extracted, yet the table sometimes assigns fairly strong confidence to the underlying result. The text does acknowledge these limits, but should make them visible at each claim. The main transfer-distance cautions are substantively sound, though the terminology occasionally turns a multidimensional taxonomy into a rough “farther means greater claim” ladder.

## Strengths
- The opening scope explicitly distinguishes practiced-item retention, unpracticed items, changed representations/structures, contexts, and delayed application.
- The evidence table separates observed results from instructional inference and often names design, sample, delay, outcome format, limitations, and qualitative confidence.
- The draft warns that Pan & Rickard’s pooled results are not broad far-transfer estimates and correctly scopes Alfieri’s conditional subgroup.
- The market/school discussion resists causal overreach and notes differences in format/familiarity.
- The review identifies itself as targeted, not preregistered systematic, and discloses access/extraction gaps.

## Findings and recommendations

1. **Major: Correct Nunes et al. source.** The published title is “Children’s arithmetic skills do not transfer between applied and academic mathematics.” Use publisher-matched authors and bibliographic metadata. This was subsequently checked on the Nature source page; see verification log.
2. **Major: Tie each exact quantitative result to its access level.** Alfieri subgroup results, Gentner percentages, and Nunes figures need explicit provenance (full-text page/table, abstract/index, or search-level) and confidence calibrated to that provenance. Do not allow precision to imply full-text verification. Add locators where available.
3. **Major: Contain the Rittle-Johnson identity/access gap.** Verify DOI/title against publisher metadata and an independent index; if the full paper remains inaccessible, say so and constrain the result to the accessible abstract/index. Do not imply the DOI is invalid based on a failed lookup.
4. **Important: Avoid a monotonic transfer-distance scale.** Replace “farther dimensions … greater claim” with a requirement to specify and justify changed dimensions. Define “near” as an illustrative, nonuniversal heuristic or keep only study-specific labels.
5. **Important: Complete table audit fields.** Where age, denominator, exact test/delay, uncertainty, sample or locator is unavailable, mark whether not reported, not retrieved, or not extracted. Avoid unsupported confidence.
6. **Important: Map a boundary/contrary result.** Sala et al. (2019) should have a concise separate entry with population/domain, comparator, outcome and limitations—or be explicitly excluded. Do not treat cognitive-training evidence as an estimate for curriculum/example comparison.
7. **Minor: Define confidence.** It should mean support for the row’s stated description given its source/access level, not empirical certainty that transfer occurs or a formal risk-of-bias grade.

## Independent review caveat

This reviewer did not fetch external source pages; source-specific factual checks rely on the supplied verifier findings and draft. Verdict: useful, cautious targeted synthesis, but not yet fully audit-ready before the corrections above. Confidence in this critique is moderate.
