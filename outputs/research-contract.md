# Research Contract

For this project, investigate: What does the strongest available evidence show about how humans acquire, understand, retain, retrieve, generalize, and apply knowledge and skills, and how should that evidence guide an adaptive learning engine?

Save a short research contract at outputs/research-contract.md and use it in subsequent runs in this project.

For every important claim, record:
- intervention or exposure AND comparator;
- outcome: immediate performance, delayed retention, understanding, transfer, or procedural fluency;
- population, age group, subject domain, setting, measurement, and delay;
- study design, sample size or number of studies, effect estimate if reported, and uncertainty;
- supporting source with DOI, stable identifier, or direct link;
- limitations, contrary evidence, moderators, replication status, and confidence;
- observed result separately from proposed cognitive or biological mechanism;
- implications for learner modeling or instruction, labeled as inference where appropriate.

Prioritize strong systematic reviews and meta-analyses for mapping a field, but check their included studies, heterogeneity, publication bias, and applicability. Also inspect key primary experiments, replications, and classroom studies. Do not treat an evidence hierarchy as a substitute for judging study quality. Do not invent effect sizes or citations. Label inaccessible papers and unverified claims. Compare competing explanations fairly. Do not generalize findings from adults to adolescents or from laboratory recall tests to classroom understanding without evidence.

Keep an evidence ledger and source trail. Write disagreements and missing evidence explicitly. Preserve intermediate artifacts and provenance where your workflow supports them.
