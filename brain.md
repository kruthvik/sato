# brain.md

<!--
  This file belongs to the learner. The engine reads it automatically at the start
  of every session and uses it to shape how things are explained.

  What actually reaches the tutor:
    - Text inside HTML comment markers, like this block, is ignored.
    - Every heading line is ignored. Only your own sentences survive.
    - The footer under the last horizontal rule is ignored.
    - While this file is still blank, the engine claims no preferences at all
      and simply starts from your first message.

  Nothing here is a setting, a credential, or a grade. Delete anything that stops
  being true.
-->

Start here. Write in your own words. Every prompt below is optional and nothing is scored. Lines that no longer describe you are worse than no line at all, so cut them.

## First, know which half you are writing in

<!--
  Not every sentence you can write here carries the same authority. The split
  below is the single most useful thing to understand about this file.

  HALF A - Delivery. Honoured as written, immediately, with no argument.
  HALF B - Judgement about yourself. Recorded as self-report, then checked
           against performance the engine can actually observe. The engine is
           allowed to overrule you, and it will.

  Observed independent performance outranks everything you write in Half B.
  A confident sentence here never becomes a grade, a shortcut, or a pass.
-->

## Half A: how you want material delivered

<!--
  Ask yourself: what should the tutor do differently on the first try, so that
  the idea actually lands? Be concrete and behavioural. "Shorter paragraphs" is
  usable. "Explain it properly" is not, because nobody can act on it.

  Some prompts worth considering, in rough order of usefulness:
    - What should an explanation open with, before any detail?
    - How much can you absorb in one sitting before it stops being useful?
    - Which representation actually works: a diagram, a worked example, a
      contrast between two similar cases, a physical or spatial picture, a
      formula, a real worked problem, a spoken-style walkthrough?
    - What should the tutor refuse to do, because it wastes your time?
    - When you are stuck, what unsticks you: another example, a different
      angle, a harder question, a break, or being asked to explain it back?
    - How do you want to be corrected when you are wrong?
    - How much do you want to be challenged versus reassured?
    - How long should a single session run before you want to stop?
    - What language and notation should it default to?
    - When two approaches are both defensible, which should it pick?

  Write as many or as few of these as you like. Leave the rest blank.
-->

## Half B: your judgement about your own understanding

<!--
  This half is worth writing, but read this first.

  Everything here is treated as a hypothesis, never as a fact about you. The
  engine will act on it as a starting point and then test it against what you
  actually produce. If your self-assessment and your performance disagree, the
  performance is what counts.

  So write honestly rather than aspirationally. A line that claims you already
  know something will be checked, and being wrong about it is the fastest way to
  get that topic re-taught from the beginning.

  Some prompts worth considering:
    - Which subjects or families of ideas you already meet comfortably, and at
      what depth: fluent use, working familiarity, or recognition only?
    - Where you tend to plateau, or stall without noticing.
    - Which errors you know you make repeatedly.
    - What "understanding it" means to you, concretely. What would you be able
      to do that you could not do before?
    - What evidence would convince you that you have actually got it?
    - What you have tried before that did not work for you.
    - Where you would rather be corrected now than later, even if it is
      uncomfortable, and where you would rather not be interrupted.

  Note that mastery, readiness labels, review scheduling, and assessment
  decisions are never yours to set here. This section only tells the tutor where
  to push and where to start.
-->

## What this file deliberately cannot do

<!--
  Listed so you do not spend effort on requests that will be ignored, and so you
  know the tutor is not simply failing to read your message.

    - It cannot grant you a mastery level, a percentage, or a pass.
    - It cannot mark a topic as learned, exempt you from assessment, or shorten
      the evidence the engine requires before calling something understood.
    - It cannot weaken, skip, or reveal the conditions of an assessment.
    - It cannot change safety handling, answer-key protection, or how delayed
      review is scheduled.
    - It cannot suppress an honest report that you are not ready.

  Delivery is yours. Evidence is the engine's. Keeping that line clear is what
  makes the progress it reports worth anything to you.
-->

## Keeping it honest

<!--
  A useful routine, not a chore:

    - Add a line the moment a session goes badly or unusually well.
    - Rewrite a line the moment it stops predicting how you actually learn.
    - Delete preferences that were only ever polite, aspirational, or copied.
    - Once per subject, not once per session. This file should stay short
      enough to read in under a minute, or you will stop trusting it.

  Short and accurate beats long and aspirational. Ten true sentences will steer
  this engine better than a page of good intentions.
-->

---
**For a new session:** You do not need to ask for this file to be read. It is loaded for you at the start of every session, and it is read as self-report only - never as evidence of what you know, and never as a way to bypass assessment. Leave it blank and the engine will simply ask you what you want to work on.
